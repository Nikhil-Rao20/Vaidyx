## DENGUE FEVER — NVBDCP/ICMR Protocol

### Classification (WHO 2009)
- **Dengue without warning signs:** Fever + 2 of (nausea/vomiting, rash, aches, leukopenia, positive tourniquet test)
- **Dengue with warning signs (Group B):** Abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, clinical fluid accumulation, mucosal bleed, lethargy, liver >2 cm, rising haematocrit + rapid platelet fall
- **Severe Dengue (Group C):** Severe plasma leakage, severe bleed, severe organ impairment

### Management — Group A (Outpatient)
- **Paracetamol (Crocin, Dolo 650)** 15 mg/kg q4–6h PRN fever — ONLY antipyretic allowed
- Oral fluids: 1.5–2 L/day (ORS, coconut water, juices)
- **STRICT AVOID:** Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac — risk of Reye's syndrome + increased bleeding; BANNED by MOHFW in dengue
- Review daily; admit if warning signs or platelet <100,000/µL
- Platelet transfusion: ONLY if <10,000/µL or active bleed with <20,000/µL (ICMR 2015)

### Management — Group B (Inpatient)
- IV fluids: 0.9% NS or Ringer's Lactate
  - Warning signs, can tolerate oral: 2–3 mL/kg/hr IV
  - Compensated shock: 5–10 mL/kg IV over 1h; reassess
- Monitor: haematocrit q4–6h, urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr
- Transfusion: packed RBC if haemoglobin <7 g/dL with haemodynamic instability

### Management — Group C (ICU — Dengue Shock Syndrome, DSS)
- Crystalloid: 20 mL/kg 0.9% NS over 15–30 min → reassess
- Colloid (Gelofusine, Albumin 4%) if haematocrit rising despite crystalloid
- ICU monitoring: CVP, urine output, serial haematocrit

### Source: NVBDCP Dengue Management Guidelines (2021), ICMR

## MALARIA — NVBDCP India Protocol

### Diagnosis First
- Peripheral smear (gold standard) + RDT (PfHRP2/pLDH based)
- India has both P. vivax (dominant in most states) and P. falciparum (dominant in Northeast, tribal areas, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand)

### P. VIVAX — Uncomplicated
- **Chloroquine (Lariago, Resochin)** 25 mg base/kg over 3 days:
  - Day 1: 10 mg/kg (max 600 mg base)
  - Day 2: 10 mg/kg
  - Day 3: 5 mg/kg
- **PLUS Primaquine (Primaquine IP)** 0.25 mg/kg OD × 14 days for radical cure
  - SCREEN FOR G6PD DEFICIENCY BEFORE PRIMAQUINE — risk of haemolytic anaemia
  - Contraindicated: G6PD deficiency, pregnancy, infants <6 months

### P. FALCIPARUM — Uncomplicated
- **Artemether-Lumefantrine (Coartem, AL-Combi)** — FIRST-LINE (NVBDCP 2021):
  - <15 kg: 1 tablet BD × 3 days; 15–25 kg: 2 tablets BD; 25–35 kg: 3 tablets BD; >35 kg: 4 tablets BD
  - Take with food; AVOID in 1st trimester
- **PLUS Primaquine (single dose)** 0.75 mg/kg (max 45 mg) on Day 1 for gametocyte clearance
  - Note: Single dose primaquine for falciparum (unlike 14-day for vivax)
- **Alternative:** Artesunate-Sulfadoxine-Pyrimethamine (AS+SP) — used in some regions

### SEVERE FALCIPARUM MALARIA — ICU Protocol (NVBDCP)
- **IV Artesunate (Falcigo, Injectartesunate)** — FIRST-LINE for severe malaria in India:
  - 2.4 mg/kg IV at 0h, 12h, 24h → then 2.4 mg/kg OD
  - Continue until patient can swallow → switch to oral ACT to complete 3 days
- **Alternative if artesunate unavailable:** Quinine dihydrochloride 20 mg salt/kg IV loading (over 4h in 5% dextrose) → 10 mg/kg q8h
- **Supportive:** Dextrose 10% IV (hypoglycaemia common with quinine/artesunate), blood transfusion if Hb <5 g/dL, dialysis for AKI, mechanical ventilation for ARDS

### Mixed Infection (Vivax + Falciparum)
- Treat as falciparum (ACT) + primaquine 14-day course

### Source: NVBDCP Treatment Guidelines (2021), WHO Malaria Treatment Guidelines adapted for India

## TYPHOID (Enteric Fever) — ICMR/API India Protocol

> Key India context: Extensively Drug-Resistant (XDR) typhoid emerging; fluoroquinolone resistance >90% in many Indian cities; nalidixic acid resistance = marker of fluoroquinolone failure

### First-Line (Uncomplicated Typhoid)
- **Azithromycin (Azee, Zithromax)** 1 g OD × 7 days (adults) OR 20 mg/kg OD × 7 days (children, max 1 g)
  - Preferred first-line in most Indian centres due to fluoroquinolone resistance
  - Not appropriate for severe typhoid

### Alternative First-Line
- **Cefixime (Taxim-O, Zifi)** 20 mg/kg/day in 2 divided doses × 14 days (oral cephalosporin — preferred paediatrics)
- **Ceftriaxone (Monocef, Rocephin)** 75 mg/kg/day (max 2.5 g) IV OD × 10–14 days — for complicated/severe cases

### Severe Typhoid (hospitalisation required)
- **Ceftriaxone** 75 mg/kg IV OD (max 2–4 g) × 14 days
- **Meropenem (Meromer, Meronem)** — reserve for XDR typhoid or failed cephalosporin
- **Dexamethasone (Deca-Durabolin, Dexona)** 3 mg/kg IV loading → 1 mg/kg q6h × 8 doses — for severe typhoid with altered sensorium/shock (API recommendation)

### Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Ofloxacin) — AVOID in India
- >85–90% resistance in Salmonella typhi across most Indian states
- Only use if susceptibility confirmed by culture

### Carriers
- **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg BD × 4 weeks (if susceptible) — for chronic gallbladder carriers without cholecystitis

### Source: ICMR AMR Surveillance, API Guidelines for Typhoid, AIIMS Protocols

## SCRUB TYPHUS — ICMR India Protocol

> Common in hilly/rural India (Himalayas, Northeast, Western Ghats, Nilgiris); often missed; classic triad: fever + eschar + rash (eschar found in <50%)

### First-Line
- **Doxycycline (Doxy-1, Doxt SL)** 100 mg PO BD × 7 days (adults)
  - Paediatric: 2.2 mg/kg BD × 7 days
  - Dramatic clinical improvement within 24–48h is diagnostic (therapeutic test)

### Severe/Complicated (myocarditis, meningitis, ARDS, MODS)
- **Doxycycline 100 mg IV** BD → switch to oral when stable
- **Azithromycin (Azee)** 500 mg OD × 3–5 days — alternative in pregnancy, children <8 years
- **Rifampicin (Rifadin)** 600–900 mg OD — reserve for doxycycline-refractory cases (rare)

### Avoid
- Beta-lactams, aminoglycosides — INEFFECTIVE against Orientia tsutsugamushi

### Monitor
- LFTs, renal function, echo for myocarditis, CSF if meningism present
- Thrombocytopenia common — platelet transfusion only if <10,000 or active bleed

### Source: ICMR Task Force on Scrub Typhus (2021), CMC Vellore Protocols

## LEPTOSPIROSIS — India (especially Mumbai, Kerala, Goa post-monsoon)

### Mild (Anicteric, Outpatient)
- **Doxycycline (Doxy-1)** 100 mg PO BD × 7 days

### Moderate-Severe (Hospitalised)
- **Penicillin G (Crystalline Penicillin)** 1.5 million units IV q6h × 7 days — FIRST-LINE for severe leptospirosis
- **Ampicillin** 500–1000 mg IV q6h × 7 days — alternative
- **Ceftriaxone (Monocef)** 1 g IV OD × 7 days — easy administration, good efficacy

### Severe (Weil's Disease: AKI + jaundice + bleeding)
- **Penicillin G** high-dose + dialysis support for AKI
- Platelet transfusion for bleeding diathesis
- Penicillin G + Doxycycline combination in some Indian centres

### Prophylaxis (post-exposure, high-risk groups)
- **Doxycycline** 200 mg PO once weekly during exposure period

### Source: ICMR, Government of India Leptospirosis Control Guidelines

## JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS (JE) — India

> Endemic: Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Assam, West Bengal (annual outbreaks)

### Treatment
- **Supportive only** — no specific antiviral available
- Phenobarbitone 10–20 mg/kg IV for seizure (preferred over phenytoin — less cardiotoxicity in JE patients with myocarditis)
- Mannitol 0.25–0.5 g/kg IV for raised ICP
- Fluid restriction (SIADH common)

### Prevention
- **JE vaccine (Jenvac — inactivated SA14-14-2)** — Indian-made; part of UIP in endemic states

### Source: ICMR JE Management Guidelines, NVBDCP

## COVID-19 Complications — Indian Protocols (Residual Guidance)

### Mucormycosis (Black Fungus) — Post-COVID
- **Liposomal Amphotericin B (AmBisome, Fungisome)** 5–10 mg/kg/day IV — FIRST-LINE
- **Isavuconazole** 200 mg TDS × 2 days loading → 200 mg OD — alternative/step-down
- Surgical debridement mandatory for rhino-orbital-cerebral form
- Glycaemic control critical (HbA1c target <7%)

### Source: ICMR-DGHS Management Protocol for Mucormycosis (2021)
