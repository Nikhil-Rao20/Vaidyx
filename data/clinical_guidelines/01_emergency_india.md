## STATUS EPILEPTICUS — India Protocol

### Phase 1 — Immediate (0–5 min) — Benzodiazepin

**IV access available:**
- **Lorazepam (Larpose, Ativan)** 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg/dose) at 2 mg/min
  - Repeat ONCE after 5 min if seizure continues
  - Most widely available in Indian tertiary hospitals

**No IV access (PHC/field):**
- **Diazepam (Calmpose, Valium) rectal** 0.5 mg/kg rectally (max 20 mg adults, 10 mg child)
- **Midazolam (Mezolam, Dormicum) IM** 0.2 mg/kg IM (max 10 mg adult)
  - Buccal midazolam 0.5 mg/kg (max 10 mg) — effective at PHC level

**Paediatric dose:**
- Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV; Diazepam 0.3–0.5 mg/kg rectal; Midazolam 0.2–0.3 mg/kg buccal/IN

### Phase 2 — Urgent (5–20 min, benzo failed)

- **Phenytoin (Dilantin, Eptoin)** 18–20 mg/kg IV at ≤50 mg/min in 0.9% NaCl (NOT dextrose — precipitates); monitor ECG and BP
- **Fosphenytoin (Cerebyx)** 20 mg PE/kg IV at ≤150 mg PE/min — faster, safer; available in Indian tertiary centres
- **Levetiracetam (Levipil, Keppra)** 60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg) over 10 min — preferred in hepatic dysfunction, pregnancy, paediatrics; increasingly available in India
- **Valproate sodium (Valparin, Epilim IV)** 30–40 mg/kg IV over 10–15 min — avoid in liver disease, pregnancy, <2 years

### Phase 3 — Refractory SE (>30 min) — ICU

- **Thiopentone (Thiopental)** 3–5 mg/kg IV → 3–5 mg/kg/hr infusion — available in Indian ICUs; titrate to EEG burst-suppression
- **Propofol (Profol, Diprivan)** 1–2 mg/kg IV → 20–200 mcg/kg/min infusion
- **Midazolam infusion** 0.2 mg/kg loading → 0.05–2 mg/kg/hr

### Adjunct — Fever in SE

- **Paracetamol (Crocin, Dolo 650)** 15 mg/kg IV/PO q6h (max 4 g/day adult)
- Tepid sponging + fan — important in resource-limited settings
- Check blood glucose — give 25% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV if hypoglycaemic

### Monitor
- SpO2, RR, BP q5 min; have airway trolley ready
- Electrolytes (Na, Ca, Mg) — correct aberrations
- CT brain if new-onset or refractory

### Source: Neurocritical Care Society of India, API Textbook of Medicine (10th Ed), IAP Guidelines

## STEMI — ACUTE MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (Indian Context)

> Note: Many Indian patients present late (>12h). Fibrinolysis is often the only option at district hospitals; cath labs are concentrated in tier-1 cities.

### Immediate — ALL STEMI (door-to-drug within 30 min if fibrinolysis; door-to-balloon ≤90 min if PCI)

1. **Aspirin (Ecosprin, Loprin)** 325 mg PO stat (crushed/chewed) → 75 mg OD maintenance
2. **Clopidogrel (Clopilet, Plavix)** — P2Y12 of choice in India due to cost:
   - If fibrinolysis planned: 300 mg loading (age <75), 75 mg loading (age ≥75)
   - If PCI: 600 mg loading
   - Note: Ticagrelor (Brilinta, Torcab) 180 mg preferred if PCI and cost allows
3. **Anticoagulation:**
   - **Heparin (Hep-Lock, Inj. Heparin)** UFH 60 units/kg IV bolus (max 4000 units) → 12 units/kg/hr (max 1000 units/hr); target aPTT 60–80 sec
   - **Enoxaparin (Clexane, Emclot)** 1 mg/kg SC q12h — if fibrinolysis, give 30 mg IV bolus first (age <75, CrCl ≥30)
4. **Oxygen** only if SpO2 <94%
5. **Sublingual Nitroglycerin (Sorbitrate SL, Nitrostat)** 0.4–0.5 mg SL q5 min × 3 PRN chest pain — AVOID if SBP <90, RV infarct, sildenafil <48h

### Fibrinolysis — District Hospital Protocol (if PCI not within 120 min)

- **Streptokinase (Streptase)** 1.5 million units IV over 60 min in 100 mL NS — most affordable, widely available in India; premedicate with hydrocortisone 100 mg IV
- **Tenecteplase (Metalyse, TNKase)** weight-based single bolus (preferred in India for ease of use):
  - <60 kg: 30 mg; 60–70 kg: 35 mg; 70–80 kg: 40 mg; 80–90 kg: 45 mg; >90 kg: 50 mg
- **Alteplase (Actilyse)** 15 mg bolus → 0.75 mg/kg over 30 min → 0.5 mg/kg over 60 min (total max 100 mg)

### Absolute Contraindications to Thrombolysis
Prior ICH, ischemic stroke <3 months, active internal bleed, uncontrolled BP >180/110 at presentation, suspected aortic dissection, significant head trauma <3 months

### Post-fibrinolysis Transfer
Transfer to PCI-capable centre within 3–24h for pharmacoinvasive strategy (Indian guideline recommendation)

### Source: Cardiological Society of India (CSI) STEMI Guidelines (2020), API, ACC/AHA adapted for Indian context

## ANAPHYLAXIS — India Protocol

### First-Line (ALWAYS first — do NOT delay)
- **Adrenaline/Epinephrine (Adrenaline IP, Adrenex)** 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1:1000) IM anterolateral thigh — adult
  - Paediatric: 0.01 mg/kg IM (max 0.5 mg)
  - Repeat every 5–15 min — most patients need 1–2 doses
  - If IV available in severe: 50–100 mcg IV (dilute 1 mL 1:1000 to 10 mL → give 0.5–1 mL slow IV)

### Second-Line (after adrenaline)
- **IV fluids** 1–2 L 0.9% NS rapid bolus for hypotension
- **Salbutamol (Asthalin, Ventolin)** nebulised 2.5–5 mg for bronchospasm
- **Pheniramine (Avil)** 22.75 mg IV/IM slow — available in every Indian hospital
- **Hydrocortisone (Solu-Cortef, Efcorlin)** 200 mg IV — delayed onset 4–6h but prevents biphasic reaction
- **Glucagon (GlucaGen)** 1–2 mg IV if on beta-blocker — often unavailable at PHC; have adrenaline infusion ready

### Monitor
- Observe minimum 6–8h after resolution; 24h if severe/biphasic risk
- Identify and document trigger for future prevention

### Source: Indian College of Allergy and Applied Immunology, WAO Guidelines adapted

## HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY — India

### Definition
SBP >180 mmHg AND/OR DBP >120 mmHg WITH end-organ damage

### Target
Reduce MAP by ≤25% in first hour. DO NOT normalize rapidly (ischemia risk).

### IV Agents Available in India
- **Labetalol (Labebet, Trandate)** 20 mg IV slow over 2 min → 20–80 mg q10 min (max 300 mg) — preferred; available in Indian hospitals
- **Hydralazine (Nepresol, Hydralazine IP)** 5–10 mg IV slow q20 min — obstetric hypertension/eclampsia
- **Sodium Nitroprusside (Sodium Nitroprusside IP)** 0.3–0.5 mcg/kg/min → titrate (max 10 mcg/kg/min) — ICU only; monitor cyanide toxicity
- **Nicardipine** 5 mg/hr IV → titrate — less commonly available outside metro hospitals
- **Oral agents if mild/moderate (no ICU):** Amlodipine (Amlopress, Stamlo) 5–10 mg PO + Clinidipine (Clinidipine IP) or Metoprolol (Metocor, Betaloc) 25–50 mg PO

### Avoid
- Sublingual nifedipine (uncontrolled rapid drop — BANNED from this use in many Indian guidelines)
- Rapid normalization of BP

### Eclampsia/Severe Pre-eclampsia
- **Magnesium Sulphate (Mag Sulph IP)** 4 g IV loading over 20 min → 1–2 g/hr maintenance — FIRST-LINE for seizure prophylaxis/treatment in eclampsia
- **Hydralazine** 5 mg IV q20 min OR Labetalol for BP control
- **Nifedipine (Depin, Adalat)** 10 mg PO q20 min if IV not available

### Source: FOGSI (Federation of Obstetric and Gynaecological Societies of India), API, Indian Hypertension Guidelines (IHS 2023)

## DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (DKA) — India

### Fluid (most critical step)
- 0.9% NS 1 L in first hour → 1 L over next 2h → 1 L over next 4h → 1 L over next 8h
- Switch to 0.45% NS when Na corrected; switch to 5% Dextrose when BGL <200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)

### Insulin
- **Regular insulin (Actrapid, Humulin R)** 0.1 units/kg/hr IV infusion — DO NOT give bolus
- Target BGL fall 50–75 mg/dL/hour; if not falling by 50 mg/dL in first hour → double rate
- Switch to SC insulin when: BGL <200, pH >7.3, HCO3 >15, patient eating

### Potassium (CRITICAL)
- K <3.5: Replace KCl 20–40 mEq/h IV BEFORE starting insulin
- K 3.5–5.0: Add 20–30 mEq KCl per litre of fluid with insulin
- K >5.0 or no urine: Hold potassium, monitor q2h
- KCl (Potassium Chloride IP) widely available in all Indian hospitals

### Bicarbonate
- Only if pH <6.9: Sodium Bicarbonate 100 mEq IV over 2h
- Do NOT use routinely — worsens cellular acidosis

### Source: RSSDI (Research Society for Study of Diabetes in India) Guidelines (2022), API
