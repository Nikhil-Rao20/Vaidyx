## DIABETES MELLITUS TYPE 2 — RSSDI Guidelines India (2023)

### Diagnosis (ADA/RSSDI)
- FPG ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) OR 2h PG ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) on OGTT OR HbA1c ≥6.5% OR random PG ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms

### Targets (RSSDI India)
- HbA1c: <7.0% (general); <6.5% (young, short duration, no CVD); <8.0% (elderly, CKD, CVD history)
- Pre-meal glucose: 80–130 mg/dL; Post-meal 2h: <180 mg/dL
- BP: <130/80 mmHg; LDL: <70 mg/dL (if CVD) / <100 mg/dL (no CVD)

### Treatment Algorithm

**Step 1 — Lifestyle + Metformin**
- **Metformin (Glycomet, Glucophage)** 500 mg OD with dinner → titrate up to 2 g/day (BD dosing)
- Lifestyle: low GI diet, 150 min moderate exercise/week

**Step 2 — Add second agent (HbA1c not at target after 3 months)**
- Preferred add-ons in India (RSSDI 2023):
  - **SGLT2 inhibitor** (Empagliflozin/Jardiance 10–25 mg OD or Dapagliflozin/Forxiga 10 mg OD) — if CVD, heart failure, or CKD (eGFR ≥30)
  - **GLP-1 agonist** (Liraglutide/Victoza 0.6→1.8 mg SC OD or Semaglutide/Rybelsus oral 7–14 mg OD) — if CVD or obesity
  - **DPP-4 inhibitor** (Sitagliptin/Januvia 100 mg OD) — safe, weight-neutral; reduce dose CrCl<50
  - **Sulfonylurea** (Glimepiride/Amaryl 1–4 mg OD or Glibenclamide/Daonil 5–10 mg OD) — low cost; hypoglycaemia risk
  - **Pioglitazone** 15–30 mg OD — effective but fluid retention, bladder cancer risk; use with caution

**Step 3 — Triple therapy or Basal Insulin**
- Add basal insulin (Glargine/Lantus or Basalog 0.1–0.2 units/kg OD bedtime) to oral agents
- Titrate by 2 units every 3 days to FPG target

**Step 4 — Intensify insulin (Basal-Bolus)**
- Add rapid-acting (Aspart/Novorapid or Regular/Actrapid) before meals
- Total daily dose: 0.3–0.5 units/kg/day initially

### Complication Screening
- Diabetic nephropathy: UACR annually; use ACE inhibitor (Ramipril/Cardace 5–10 mg) or ARB if microalbuminuria
- Retinopathy: Annual fundoscopy
- Neuropathy: Annual foot exam, monofilament test
- CVD: 10-year risk assessment; statin in all T2DM with CVD risk or >40 years

### Indian-Specific Considerations
- Indians develop DM at lower BMI (~23 kg/m² threshold vs 27 in Caucasians) — Indian-specific BMI cut-offs used
- Lean T2DM common in India — MODY variants more prevalent
- Hepatic steatosis (NAFLD) co-exists in 60–80% of Indian T2DM
- Glycaemic variability significant — CGM increasingly recommended for insulin users

## HYPERTENSION — Indian Hypertension Guidelines (IHS 2023)

### Classification (IHS 2023)
- Normal: SBP <120 and DBP <80
- Elevated: SBP 120–129 and DBP <80
- Stage 1 HTN: SBP 130–139 OR DBP 80–89
- Stage 2 HTN: SBP ≥140 OR DBP ≥90
- Hypertensive Crisis: SBP >180 and/or DBP >120

### BP Targets
- General adult: <130/80 mmHg
- Age >75 years/frail: <140/90 mmHg
- DM + HTN: <130/80 mmHg
- CKD + proteinuria: <130/80 mmHg

### Treatment Algorithm (IHS 2023)

**First-Line (all monotherapy options equal):**
- **CCB:** Amlodipine (Amlopress, Stamlo) 5–10 mg OD — preferred first-line in Indians (high sodium diet, low-renin HTN pattern)
- **ARB:** Telmisartan (Telma, Telsartan) 40–80 mg OD; Losartan (Esar, Losacar) 50–100 mg OD
- **ACEI:** Ramipril (Cardace, Hopace) 2.5–10 mg OD; Enalapril (Envas) 5–20 mg BD
- **Thiazide/Thiazide-like:** Hydrochlorothiazide (Esidrex) 12.5–25 mg OD; Chlorthalidone (Hygroton) 12.5–25 mg OD — more potent than HCTZ

**Beta-blockers** — NOT first-line for uncomplicated HTN; use if comorbid CAD, heart failure, tachyarrhythmia, pregnancy

**Combination (Stage 2 or inadequate response):**
- **CCB + ARB** (Amlopress AT = Amlodipine + Telmisartan) — most used combination in India
- **CCB + ACEI** (Amlopress Plus = Amlodipine + Ramipril)
- **CCB + ARB + Thiazide** for triple therapy

**Resistant HTN (≥3 drugs including diuretic):**
- **Spironolactone (Aldactone)** 25–50 mg OD — most effective add-on (primary aldosteronism common)
- Renal denervation — emerging in India (AIIMS, PGI, Medanta)

### Source: Indian Hypertension Society (IHS) Guidelines 2023, API

## ASTHMA — India (GINA adapted, API/IRS Guidelines)

> India: ~37 million asthmatic patients; high burden; often under-diagnosed/under-treated

### Severity Classification & Step-Up Approach

**Step 1 — Mild Intermittent (as-needed reliever)**
- **Salbutamol (Asthalin MDI)** 100 mcg/puff — 1–2 puffs PRN (short-acting bronchodilator)

**Step 2 — Mild Persistent (Low-dose ICS)**
- **Budesonide (Budecort) 100–200 mcg BD** OR **Beclomethasone (Beclate) 100 mcg BD** inhaled
- Montelukast (Montair) 10 mg OD — alternative if ICS not tolerated (common in India)

**Step 3 — Moderate Persistent (Low-dose ICS + LABA)**
- **Budesonide + Formoterol (Foracort 200 MDI/Turbuhaler)** 2 puffs BD — MOST USED combination in India (LABA + ICS)
- OR Fluticasone (Flixotide) + Salmeterol (Seretide) — available but expensive
- Continue as-needed salbutamol

**Step 4 — Severe (Medium-High ICS + LABA)**
- **Budesonide 400–800 mcg + Formoterol 12 mcg BD** (Foracort 400)
- Add Montelukast 10 mg OD, Tiotropium 18 mcg OD if uncontrolled

**Step 5 — Very Severe**
- Add low-dose oral prednisolone (5–10 mg OD)
- **Biologics** (Mepolizumab, Benralizumab, Dupilumab) — for eosinophilic severe asthma; available in India (expensive; some covered under insurance)

### Acute Severe Asthma (Emergency)
- **Salbutamol 5 mg + Ipratropium 0.5 mg nebulised q20 min × 3** — first-line acute
- **Prednisolone (Omnacortil) 1 mg/kg PO** (max 50 mg) or IV methylprednisolone if not tolerated
- **Magnesium sulphate 2 g IV** over 20 min if severe/life-threatening (PaO2 <60, PEFR <50%)
- IV Aminophylline 5 mg/kg loading (over 20 min) → 0.5 mg/kg/h infusion — Indian practice; less commonly used globally but still used here
- Oxygen target SpO2 94–98%

### Source: GINA (2023 adapted for India), Indian Respiratory Society (IRS), API Guidelines

## CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD) — India

> India: 17% CKD prevalence; DM and HTN cause 60% of ESRD; dialysis access limited; many reach ESRD undiagnosed

### Management by CKD Stage (KDIGO + Indian Nephrology Society)

**CKD G1–G2 (eGFR >60):**
- BP target: <130/80; ACE/ARB if proteinuria
- Protein restriction: 0.8 g/kg/day
- Avoid nephrotoxins: NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast (use NGDR protocol)
- **Metformin** continue if eGFR ≥45; halve dose 30–44; STOP <30

**CKD G3a–G3b (eGFR 30–59):**
- Monitor K+ monthly (hyperK risk with ACE/ARB/K-sparing diuretic)
- Anaemia: Check iron stores; **IV Ferric carboxymaltose (Ferric Carboxymaltose, Jectofer)** if iron-deficient; **Erythropoietin (Wepox, Eprex)** 50–100 units/kg SC 3×/week if Hb <10 g/dL
- Bone disease (CKD-MBD): Restrict dietary phosphate; Sevelamer (Renvela) 800 mg TDS with meals; Calcitriol (Rocaltrol) 0.25 mcg OD

**CKD G4–G5 (eGFR <30):**
- Dialysis preparation: AV fistula (6 months before HD predicted)
- Dietary: K+ <2g/day, Na <2g/day, fluid restriction
- **Dialysis indications** (ESRD India): Symptoms of uraemia, K+ >6.5 uncontrolled, refractory fluid overload, pericarditis

**Contrast Nephropathy Prevention:**
- Stop Metformin 48h before; IV 0.9% NS 1 mL/kg/h × 12h pre/post procedure; use iso-osmolar contrast (Iodixanol/Visipaque); N-acetylcysteine 600 mg BD day before and day of procedure (limited evidence but widely used in India)

### Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) in India
- Most common causes: Gastroenteritis + dehydration, Sepsis, Drugs (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast), Leptospirosis, Malaria (P. falciparum), Rhabdomyolysis (heat stroke, OP poisoning)
- Fluid resuscitation with 0.9% NS or Ringer's Lactate
- Furosemide: NOT to convert oliguric to non-oliguric AKI; only for fluid overload
- Dialysis if AEIOU: Acidosis (pH <7.1), Electrolyte (K+>6.5), Intoxication, Overload, Uraemic symptoms

### Source: Indian Society of Nephrology (ISN), KDIGO adapted, AIIMS, CMC Vellore Protocols

## STROKE — India (Hyperacute Management)

> India: Second-leading cause of death; thrombolysis under-utilised (poor access to neuroimaging, stroke centres outside metros); 80% ischaemic stroke

### Hyperacute Ischaemic Stroke — IV Thrombolysis (within 4.5h of onset)

**Eligibility (simplified):**
- NIHSS ≥4, age ≥18, symptom onset <4.5h confirmed, CT/MRI showing no haemorrhage
- BP <185/110 before thrombolysis (treat if above: Labetalol 10–20 mg IV)

**Alteplase (Actilyse)** 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) IV:
- 10% of total dose as IV bolus over 1 min
- Remaining 90% as IV infusion over 60 min
- HOLD all antiplatelets/anticoagulants × 24h post-tPA
- Admit ICU; BP target <180/105 for 24h post-tPA

**Indian access barriers:** IV tPA available only in ~200 hospitals in India; majority reach hospital >4.5h; drip-and-ship model implemented in some states

**If beyond 4.5h or tPA contraindicated:**
- **Aspirin (Ecosprin) 300 mg** stat + **Atorvastatin 40–80 mg** stat
- Clopidogrel 300 mg loading if moderate stroke (NIHSS 3–15) in first 24h → 75 mg OD × 21 days then as per cardiology
- Dual antiplatelet (Aspirin + Clopidogrel) × 21 days for TIA/minor stroke — POINT/CHANCE trial evidence

**BP Management in Acute Stroke:**
- Ischaemic: Do NOT lower BP unless >220/120 (or >185/110 if tPA candidate) — permissive hypertension
- Haemorrhagic: Target SBP 140–150 (Labetalol or Nicardipine IV)

### Source: Neurological Society of India (NSI), IAN (Indian Academy of Neurology), WHO Stroke Management
