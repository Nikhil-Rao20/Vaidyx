# Source: ICMR, AIIMS Toxicology Unit, WHO IPCS, National Poison Information Centre (NPIC) India

## ORGANOPHOSPHATE (OP) POISONING — India (Most Common Poisoning)

> India accounts for ~30% of global OP poisoning deaths. Agricultural pesticides (Chlorpyrifos, Monocrotophos, Dimethoate, Parathion) and household insecticides. Suicide is the most common mechanism in India.

### Clinical Recognition — SLUD + DUMBELS
- **Muscarinic:** Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defaecation, GI cramps, Emesis (SLUDGE); Bronchospasm, Bradycardia, Miosis, Sweating
- **Nicotinic:** Muscle fasciculations, weakness, respiratory muscle paralysis
- **CNS:** Confusion, coma, seizures

### Grading (Dreisbach/AIIMS Modified)
- **Grade 1 (Mild):** Miosis, mild salivation, nausea — pupil <3 mm, conscious
- **Grade 2 (Moderate):** Bronchorrhoea, excessive secretions, vomiting, bradycardia
- **Grade 3 (Severe):** Coma, respiratory failure, pulmonary oedema, seizures

### Treatment Protocol

#### Step 1 — Atropine (MOST CRITICAL — EARLY, ADEQUATE DOSE)
- **Atropine Sulphate (Atropine IP)** — ALL hospitals in India have this
- **Mild (Grade 1):** 1–2 mg IV bolus; repeat q5–10 min until dry secretions (atropinisation)
- **Moderate (Grade 2):** 2–4 mg IV; double dose every 5 min if no response
- **Severe (Grade 3):** 4–6 mg IV bolus; escalate rapidly; some cases need 20–100 mg total in first hour
- **End-point of atropinisation:** DRY secretions + HR >80 + clear chest (NOT pupil dilatation alone)
- **NEVER use atropine eye drops** as substitute for IV — inadequate
- Atropine infusion: After initial stabilisation, 10–20% of loading dose/hr

#### Step 2 — Pralidoxime/PAM (within FIRST 4 HOURS for maximal effect)
- **Pralidoxime Chloride (P-2-AM, Pralidox)** 1–2 g IV over 15–30 min (adult); 25–50 mg/kg (paediatric)
- Repeat 1 g q1h × 2 doses → then 1 g q4–6h or 250–500 mg/hr infusion × 24–48h
- **EFFECTIVENESS DECLINES >4h post-exposure** (phosphonylated AChE ages); give early
- Contraindicated in Carbamate poisoning (atropine alone sufficient for carbamates)
- Efficacy controversial in some Indian studies — but WHO and NPIC India still recommend it

#### Step 3 — Benzodiazepines for Seizures
- **Diazepam (Calmpose)** 10 mg IV for seizures; repeat as needed
- Do NOT use phenytoin as primary agent for OP seizures

#### Step 4 — Decontamination
- Remove all clothing; wash skin with soap and water
- Gastric lavage if oral ingestion <1h (airway protected)
- Activated charcoal 1 g/kg if within 1h AND conscious

### ICU/Ventilation
- Intubate early if GCS <10 or respiratory failure
- Intermediate Syndrome (IMS) — occurs Day 1–4 post acute phase; proximal limb + respiratory muscle weakness; requires mechanical ventilation until AChE regenerates
- Monitor QTc — atropine + some OPs prolong QT

### Specific OP antidotes unavailable in India:
- **Obidoxime** — not commercially available in India (PAM is the alternative)

## SNAKE BITE — India (Big 4 Venomous Snakes)

> India: ~58,000 deaths/year from snake bite (Lancet 2020 — highest globally). Big 4 responsible for most medically significant bites.

### Big 4 Indian Snakes

| Snake | Venom Type | Key Features |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Russell's Viper (Daboia russelii)** | Haemotoxic + Neurotoxic | Most deaths; incoagulable blood, AKI, local necrosis |
| **Common Krait (Bungarus caeruleus)** | Neurotoxic | Painless bite at night; descending paralysis; no local signs |
| **Indian Cobra (Naja naja)** | Neurotoxic + Cytotoxic | Ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, local necrosis |
| **Saw-Scaled Viper (Echis carinatus)** | Haemotoxic | Severe coagulopathy; scraping sound when disturbed |

### Assessment — 20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT)
- Place 2 mL fresh venous blood in clean glass tube; leave 20 min
- **If blood does not clot → viperine envenomation** (Russell's viper, Saw-scaled viper)
- Repeat q1h in all viperine bites

### Antivenom — Polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom Serum (ASVS)

**Indian Polyvalent ASVS** (VINS, Bharat Serums, Biological E.) — covers all Big 4
- **Indication:** Signs of systemic envenomation:
  - Coagulopathy (20WBCT positive or bleeding)
  - Neurotoxicity (ptosis, weakness, dysphagia, respiratory failure)
  - Cardiovascular involvement
  - AKI (oliguria, haematuria)
  - NOT local swelling/pain alone

**Dosing (MOHFW, India):**
- **Initial dose:** 10 vials (50 mL) IV in 100–200 mL 0.9% NS over 1 hour (or 4–6 vials over 30 min — faster protocol)
- **Repeat:** 10 vials if no response in 2h (coagulopathy) or 1h (neurotoxicity)
- **Total:** Up to 50–100 vials in severe cases (same dose for adults and children — venom not weight-based)
- **Test dose NOT recommended** — delays treatment; adrenaline more effective for anaphylaxis

**Premedication for Antivenom Anaphylaxis (controversial — some centres use):**
- Adrenaline 0.25 mg SC 10 min before antivenom — reduces immediate reactions (Premawardhena et al.)
- Promethazine (Phenergan) 25 mg IM or Chlorphenamine (Avil) 10 mg IV

### Neostigmine for Neurotoxic Bites (Krait, Cobra)
- **Neostigmine (Prostigmin, Neostigmine BP)** 0.04 mg/kg IM + **Atropine** 0.02 mg/kg IM (always co-administer) — reverses post-synaptic neurotoxicity of cobras; INEFFECTIVE for krait (pre-synaptic)
- Neostigmine 50 mcg/kg SC q30 min — may reduce need for mechanical ventilation for cobra bites

### Supportive Care
- Ventilate if respiratory failure (krait, cobra) — antivenom does NOT reverse established paralysis quickly
- Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) + Vitamin K for coagulopathy if antivenom insufficient
- Monitor urine output — dialysis if oliguria (Russell's viper AKI)
- Wound: DO NOT incise/suck/tourniquet — tissue damage without benefit
- Tetanus prophylaxis as indicated

### AVOID
- Incision, suction, tourniquet, ice application (worsen necrosis)
- Electric shock, herbal remedies — ineffective and harmful
- Aspirin/NSAIDs (worsen bleeding)

### Source: MOHFW (Ministry of Health & Family Welfare) Snake Bite Management Guidelines (2016), AIIMS, WHO Snake Bite Management

## SCORPION STING — India (Mesobuthus tamulus — Indian Red Scorpion)

> Most lethal scorpion in Asia; concentrated in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka

### Clinical Features
- **Autonomic storm:** Hypertension, tachycardia → then hypotension, bradycardia (cardiogenic shock)
- Myocarditis: Most common cause of death
- Pulmonary oedema (left ventricular failure)
- Hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia
- Paediatric cases: More severe, higher mortality

### Treatment — Prazosin Protocol (India-Specific)
- **Prazosin (Minipress, Hylow)** — KEY DRUG for Indian red scorpion sting
  - Adult: 250 mcg PO q3–4h until clinical improvement (reduces catecholamine surge)
  - Paediatric: 30 mcg/kg PO q3–4h
  - Note: Prazosin specific to Indian Red Scorpion toxin — not validated for all scorpion species

### Supportive Care
- IV access; monitor ECG, BP, echo
- **Dobutamine (Dobutrex)** 5–10 mcg/kg/min if cardiogenic shock (positive inotrope)
- Frusemide (Lasix) for pulmonary oedema
- Anti-scorpion venom: Limited availability in India; use if available (Institute of Sera, Haffkine, Mumbai)

### Avoid
- Opioids, sedatives (worsen respiratory depression)
- Calcium gluconate — ineffective for Indian red scorpion

### Source: AIIMS, National Institute of Occupational Health (NIOH), DF Bawaskar Protocols (Maharashtra)

## ALUMINIUM PHOSPHIDE (RICE TABLET) POISONING — India

> ALP (Celphos, Quickphos) is the most lethal pesticide ingestion in India; generates phosphine gas in stomach; extremely high case fatality rate (50–100%)

### No specific antidote exists

### Management (Supportive)
- **DO NOT induce vomiting** (volatilises phosphine gas — danger to staff)
- Gastric lavage with KMnO4 (1:10,000) or NaHCO3 solution
- **Coconut oil 250 mL PO** — may reduce phosphine absorption (Indian practice, limited evidence)
- IV fluids: 0.9% NS; avoid dextrose initially
- **Magnesium sulphate** 2–4 g IV for ventricular arrhythmia (VF/VT)
- **Sodium bicarbonate** for metabolic acidosis
- Inotropes: Dobutamine, Dopamine for cardiogenic shock
- N-acetylcysteine 150 mg/kg IV over 60 min — antioxidant support
- Mechanical ventilation early
- **Absolute avoid:** Atropine (worsens ventricular arrhythmia in ALP)

### Source: AIIMS Toxicology, PGIMER Chandigarh Protocols, NPIC India
