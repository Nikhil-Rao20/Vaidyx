# Similar Case Files (research for Phase 7)

Research for plan item 0.8 (feeds items 7.1 to 7.4). Written 27 Oct 2026. Read-only study of today's code plus web sources. Rules from `docs/night-v3/PLAN.md` D4, D8, D10 and `CLAUDE.md` (Invariants, Do not touch) are applied exactly. Nothing here is built yet.

## 1. Summary

1. Today nothing searches across Case Files: `search.CaseIndex` is one index per file and `guidelines.GuidelineIndex` is one global index of the library. Phase 7 adds a third thing, one profile per past Case File and one hospital-wide index of those profiles.
2. A profile is built by code from what is already verified (facts with quotes, confirmed stage, board records, the Case summary). It holds no name, no UHID and no day-level date in the part that is shown.
3. The real case TB-2026-bharath and archived files are never profiled. The test is a hard list check (`config.PROTECTED_CASES`, `access.is_protected`) at build time, at query time and again at card time.
4. Candidates come from a cancer-group filter, then BM25 plus the existing snowflake-arctic-embed-m-long vectors fused by reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), the same recipe as `search.CaseIndex.search`.
5. The reasoner (Gemma-4-31B, non-medical, bf16) re-orders at most 20 candidates, listwise, at temperature 0, over three fixed permutations of the list, and the three orders are merged by code (median rank, ties by RRF rank). The output is an order, never a number.
6. "Why similar" and "how it differs" lines are extractive: the model picks line ids from numbered lists of stored lines of this file and of the past file, and code copies the exact text and checks it word for word. Nothing is generated. Fewer than two surviving lines means a plainly labelled field-only card.
7. A card shows cancer, setting, stage as recorded or confirmed, key values, board decisions with month and year and intent, the outcome so far, and the label "Past Case File, not advice for this patient". It identifies the past file only by its Case File id and department until opened.
8. Opening follows D10: a file the person may already open opens normally; otherwise the break-glass dialog of item 1.9 with the reason "similar to TB-xxx", read-only, 24 h, audited, the owner's consultants told. Item 1.9 must first become possible for doctors (today only the Vaidyx admin has it).
9. Cost is small: a few hundred profiles is a few hundred short texts (seconds to embed on CPU, cached by content), one search is one query embedding plus about four reasoner calls.
10. Seven questions for the owner are in section 7. The biggest are whether the owner department shows on the card, whether undecided files are searched, and whether a doctor sees clinical text of a file outside their department before break-glass.

- **Tang et al. "Found in the Middle: Permutation Self-Consistency Improves Listwise Ranking in Large Language Models", NAACL 2024.** Listwise rankers are sensitive to the order of the candidates in the prompt; shuffling the list several times and aggregating the orders into the one closest to all of them removes most of it. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07712 . Position bias with later candidates less likely to reach the top: ECIR 2026, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03642 . Cost of mitigation is extra calls (same source).
- **Break-glass in hospitals.** Typical pattern: a warning pop-up, a stated reason, sometimes a second approval, a log of the event and of every later access; immediate review of every event is usually not feasible so reviews are sampled or periodic. Overrides were rare in one prospective study of restricted records (102 over six months). I found no study of notifying the data owner (our rule, D8, goes beyond common practice). Epic and Cerner vendor documents are not public; I did not read them.
- **Privacy law.** DPDP Act 2023 section 7 lists "certain legitimate uses" without consent (for example medical emergencies); sub-clause numbering differs between secondary sources and commencement dates are unverified, so a lawyer must check; the section is not a general permission for secondary use. https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section7.html . HIPAA Safe Harbor (a US rule, used here only as a yardstick for "de-identified"): remove names, record numbers and all date elements finer than the year, ages over 89 grouped; HHS guidance https://www.ttuhsc.edu/research/documents/hhs_deid_guidance.pdf and summaries at https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-de-identification-explained-what-you-must-remove-and-why . Our card keeps month and year (finer than Safe Harbor allows), so it is a "reduced identifiability" card, not a de-identified one; free-text quotes can still identify a patient. That is why the card is protected by quote screening and by the opening rule of D10 (section 4.10).

What the sources say, in short:
- Features used: diagnosis, stage, molecular markers, age, sex, treatments, outcomes (Manuilova 2025; Stanford service).
- How matches are shown: as a cohort or a short list with outcomes, by a person or a tool that shows its reasons (Stanford; VISTA "key differences").
- Warnings: observational data, confounding, a past treatment is not advice (Frankovich, Longhurst, Kasimir adaptation problem), position bias in model rankers (Tang), privacy of secondary use (Longhurst).
- Evaluation without ground truth: no gold standard of "similar" exists (Manuilova). The usable test is a clinician's pairwise judgement and the "would you want to see this file" question, with lists shown in shuffled order and the model's choice hidden.

## 4. The proposed pipeline

New module `tumour_board/similar.py` (item 7.1 and 7.2); routes in a new `tumour_board/api/similar.py`; card in `web/tb-nik/src/features/casefile/components/SimilarPanel.tsx` (item 7.3). Nothing in main app code.

### 4.1 What is profiled (eligibility)

A Case File is profiled when ALL hold, checked by one function `eligible(case, state)` that every other function calls:
- not protected: `case_id not in config.PROTECTED_CASES` and not `access.is_protected(case)`;
- not archived: `not access.is_archived(case)` (D4 says non-archived);
- listed: `case["listing"]["status"] == "listed"` and `protocol` or `cancer_name` set;
- decided: at least one board record with `outcome_type == "decision"` (a "past" file with a recorded decision; owner question 2 asks whether undecided files also count);
- not the file being searched from.
The test file `TB-TEST`, demo cases TB-001 to TB-003 and the synthetic history of item 7.4 are eligible (synthetic, `CLAUDE.md` e2e note). The check runs again on every result at card time, because a file may have been archived or protected since the last build.

### 4.2 Profile fields

Each profile is one JSON record, built by code, never by the model. Two parts.

Structured part (used for filters and for the card; every value copied from the record with its source):
- `case_id`, `department` (`access.owner`, kept internal; shown only if the owner says so, question 1), `cancer_group` (see 4.4), `protocol`, `cancer_name`, `setting` (`case["setting"]`), `hospital_level`.
- `stage`: list of {text, kind} with kind in `recorded` (fact `recorded_stage` or `figo_stage`, from a signed report), `confirmed_draft` (`st["staging"]["confirmed"]`, labelled "Vaidyx draft stage, confirmed by Dr X"), `agreed` (`stage_as_agreed` of a board record, item 3.1). Never computed here. No stage in any: `stage = []` and the card says "No stage recorded; none confirmed".
- `histology` (`histologic_type`, `grade`), `biomarkers` (the protocol's fact ids among `er pr her2 ki67 pdl1 egfr alk ros1 kras mmr ras_braf cea` that are verified and present), each as {fact, value, quote, doc_id, doc_title, month}. Only values with `verification in rules.VERIFIED`, `state == "present"` and a quote location (the same gate as `summary.fill_from_facts`, `summary.py:37 to 42`).
- `age_band` and `sex` from `patient.agesex` (a text such as "54 F"; parse digits and F or M or other; band in ten-year steps, "90 or older" above 89, same idea as HIPAA Safe Harbor); a value that does not parse is left empty, never guessed.
- `treatments`: `treatment_received` fact quotes (verified), `modalities` and `sequence` of board records when present.
- `decisions`: per board record `{record_id, month_year, outcome_type, intent, decision_text (whole, as recorded), rationale (first 600 characters), stage_as_agreed}` (record dates reduced to month and year for the card; full date kept in the stored profile and removed before it is sent to the browser).
- `outcome_so_far`: the sentences of the Case summary section "Treatment and follow-up since" (each already carries its record-line citation) and a code line "N records dated after the last board record; latest Mon YYYY" (count of documents with an event date after the last `board_date`, using `dates.doc_dates`). Nothing is added by the model.
- `lines`: the numbered evidence lines used for quotes (4.7): every quote above, each decision field, each summary sentence, each with `{id, source (doc_id and page, or record id and field), text}`.
- `signature`, `built_at`, `version` (profile schema version).

Never in the profile: patient name, UHID/MRN, IP/OP numbers, ABHA, accession numbers, phone, the free-text `patient` block, document file names (file names can carry names), user names of clinicians other than as the audit needs.

Text part (embedded): one plain paragraph written by code from the structured part, for example "Cancer group breast. Setting adjuvant. Age band 50 to 59. Sex F. Stage: stage as recorded IIA. Histology: invasive ductal carcinoma, grade 2. ER positive. PR positive. HER2 negative. Ki-67 20%. Treatments received: ... Board decisions: ...". The quotes are included (they are the words the BM25 side needs: "HER2 positive", "brain metastases"). Capped at about 1,500 characters; longer profiles are split into two chunks (clinical and decisions) that share the file id. No identifiers can be in it because none are in the structured part; as a second guard a scrubber removes any token equal to a patient name word or identifier of that file before embedding.

### 4.3 Index store

- Directory `data/tumour_board/similar/` (`config.DATA_ROOT / "similar"`, gitignored through `data/tumour_board/*`; never committed):
  - `profiles/<case_id>.json`: one profile per file;
  - `index.json`: chunk list `{case_id, chunk, text, cancer_group, setting, age_band, sex}` plus `version`, `embed_model`, signature of all profile signatures;
  - `index.npz`: normalised vectors, `keys` aligned with chunks.
  - `searches.jsonl`: not used (searches go to the audit logs, 4.11).
- Writes follow `guidelines.py:297 to 300` and `store._write_json`: write to a `.tmp` and `os.replace`. One process-wide lock for the build, a lazy singleton like `guidelines.index()`.
- Embedding reuses `search._embed` (lock, model, cache folder). The vector cache key is the same content hash style as `search.py:139` (`sha1(EMBED_MODEL + text)`), stored in the existing `embed_cache/chunks/` folder (shared, content keyed, so no clash). Embed in batches of 128 so the embedder lock is released between batches (as `guidelines.py:318`).
- Rebuild rules: a profile is rebuilt when its signature changes; the signature is a hash of: board record ids and their `versions` count, `artefacts.summary` or Case summary version id, confirmed stage, and the verified facts' (fact id, value) list (`rules.case_signature` already exists, used at `staging/__init__.py:420`; use it plus the board records). The index rebuilds from profiles; only changed profiles are re-embedded.
- Events that update it (one function `similar.refresh(case_id, reason)`, called from existing seams, not by editing main app code): a board decision recorded or corrected (`postboard.record_decision` and the correction path); the Case summary built or edited (item 3.4); a draft stage confirmed (`staging.confirm`); a fact confirmed or corrected (`confirm`); archive or unarchive (remove or add; `access` archive route); a Case File deleted (`store.delete_case`, remove the profile and the chunks); a re-process of documents. Each call only marks the file dirty; a single background worker (a thread, like the ready hook) debounces by 30 seconds and refreshes, so an edit never waits for an embedding. A startup scan compares signatures and fixes anything missed.
- Removal is immediate and synchronous for archive, delete and protect, so a result can never outlive its file. Query time also filters by `eligible()` (4.1) against the live case.json.

### 4.4 Candidate generation

1. Cancer-group filter (structured, code). `cancer_group` is the protocol's organ group; the registry already has one entry per checklist (`api/cases_list.py:255` lists organs by `protocol`), and Phase 2 adds 19 cancers, so the group map is read from the protocol file (`protocol.py`); colorectal rectum and colon share a group; all `generic` protocol files fall back to a normalised `cancer_name` (lower case, spelling-trimmed) and match only the same normalised name or the same synonyms list. If the group of this file is unknown, the search says so and runs on all profiles with a lower candidate cap, labelled "No cancer group on this file".
2. Optional soft filters, never hard: `setting` and `age_band` go into the text part and the query text only; they raise rank through the text match, they do not exclude. (A hard setting filter would hide a neoadjuvant file from a doctor asking about an adjuvant one that shares a mutation.) Owner question 3 covers boosting the same department.
3. Query text: the profile text part of this file, built by the same function as 4.2 (so the two sides are the same shape), plus the doctor's extra words when given. The doctor's words are appended once, as a second sentence ("Doctor's words: HER2 positive with brain metastases"), and are also run as a separate BM25 query. Effect: the words weigh in the BM25 side (exact terms) and in the dense side; a profile that quotes the words ranks higher; they never filter by themselves; they also go to the reasoner prompt as "the doctor asked to look for: ...". The words are screened as `published.check_terms` does (no name, no id, no date, a limit of 200 characters), because they are written to the audit log and to a prompt.
4. BM25 plus dense plus RRF exactly as `search.CaseIndex.search` (k1=1.4, b=0.75, RRF k=60, query prefix as line 167), over the chunks of the filtered profiles; a profile's rank is the best rank among its chunks. Keep the top 20 profiles (`N = 20`). If fewer than 3 profiles pass the filter, say so and show what there is.
5. The file's own profile and any file in the same patient's linked cases (`access.linked_owners` data, `linked_cases`) are excluded from the list or marked "linked to this file" (open question 7 asks which).

### 4.5 Re-ranking by the reasoner (no score)

- Model: Gemma-4-31B on :8100 (`llm.chat("reasoner", ...)`, `llm.py:149`), bf16 exact, non-medical, labelled so on the screen help line ("ordered by Gemma-4-31B, a non-medical model; it only orders the list").
- Input: for each candidate only its profile text part (about 200 to 300 tokens), id `P1..P20` (not the Case File id, so the model cannot be biased by id numbers and no id is leaked to the log), plus this file's profile text and the doctor's words. Total about 6,000 tokens, far below the context.
- Prompt shape (system): "You order past Case Files by how much their recorded facts resemble the current Case File: same cancer, setting, stage, histology, markers, treatments already received, and what the doctor asked for. Use only the text given. Do not judge any treatment, do not say which is better, do not say what to do. Return the ids from most to least similar, every id once." Response schema: `{"order": ["P3", "P1", ...]}` (JSON schema through the existing `json_schema` argument).
- Listwise, with position bias controlled (Tang et al.): run the same prompt over 3 fixed permutations of the 20 candidates (the candidate list in RRF order, in reverse, and in an order shuffled by a seeded generator whose seed is a hash of the file id and the list signature). Merge the three orders by median rank per candidate; ties are broken by the RRF rank. The merge is plain code, so the result is an order and no number is stored or shown. If the three orders disagree strongly (Kendall tau below 0.3), the card list still shows the merged order and nothing more; no "confidence" is invented.
- Deterministic: temperature 0 (the default in `llm.chat`), a fixed `seed` if the server supports it, fixed permutation seeds, fixed prompt version string. vLLM at temperature 0 can still differ slightly between batches, so the final order is cached under `similar/cache/<hash>.json` keyed by (file signature, candidate profile signatures, doctor's words, prompt version, model name); an identical search gets the identical order and costs nothing. A changed profile gives a new key.
- Failure: if the reasoner is down, returns an invalid id list, or a permutation fails, the list is shown in RRF order with the line "Ordered by text match only; the model ordering was not available" (the same honest-fallback habit as the rest of the product).
- Top results shown: the first 5 of the merged order, "show 5 more" up to 10 (the other 10 candidates exist only to give the model room).

### 4.6 "Why similar" and "how it differs" lines

Extractive, not generated:
1. For each of the top results, a second reasoner call (pointwise, one call per result, run in parallel) gets two numbered lists: `A1..An` lines of this file and `B1..Bm` lines of the past file (the `lines` of the two profiles, 4.2, at most 25 each, chosen by code: biomarker, histology, stage and treatment quotes first, then decision fields, then summary sentences).
2. It must answer with ids only: `{"similar": [{"a": "A3", "b": "B2"}, ...up to 3], "differs": [{"a": "A7", "b": "B5"}, ...up to 2]}`. A pair means "this line of mine and that line of theirs are the same kind of fact and agree" (similar) or "are the same kind of fact and differ" (differs). The model writes no sentence.
3. Code builds each displayed line from a fixed template and the two stored texts: for example `This file, histopathology of 12/03/2026: "invasive ductal carcinoma, grade 2". Past file, histopathology of March 2025: "invasive ductal carcinoma, grade 3".` Dates of this file are shown in full (the doctor's own file); dates of the past file are month and year.
4. Checks, all by code, before a line is kept:
   - both quotes are found word for word in the stored text they came from: this file's quote against its page text of the named document (`grounding.locate` / `grounding.norm`, `grounding.py:15 to 32, 83`) or its board record field; the past file's quote against its page text or its record field or summary sentence (profiles keep `doc_id`, `page`, so the check reads the source, not the profile copy). A quote that only fuzzily matches (score under 100, `grounding.verify`, line 94) is dropped. The same rule as "every value or sentence shown as fact needs a verified verbatim quote" in `CLAUDE.md`.
   - a quote from the past file is dropped if it contains the past patient's name words or any identifier of that file, an exact day-level date, a phone number or an email (identifier scan, same list used when building the profile);
   - a "differs" pair is dropped if the two texts are equal after `grounding.norm`; a "similar" pair is dropped if both are not about the same fact id or same section (code check on `source`), so the model cannot pair unrelated lines;
   - no line may contain the words that give advice: the fixed template contains none, and the quoted text is a record, so it is whatever the record says. A quote that is itself a treatment instruction is allowed only from the "decisions" source, where the card shows it as the past board's recorded decision, labelled; it is never offered as a "why similar" line (code: decision lines are not in the `A`/`B` lists for the similar pairs, so the explanation never repeats a past treatment as a reason).
5. If fewer than two lines survive for a result (similar and differs together), the card is still shown in its place but: shows only the surviving lines, then a plain code line "Matched by recorded fields: cancer group, setting" (values copied from the two structured parts, each with its stored quote as a small link), and the chip "No quoted reasons found". If no line survives and the structured match is the cancer group only, the result is moved to "Weaker matches" after the others. A result is never dropped for lack of lines (the doctor asked for the list), but nothing unquoted is ever shown as a reason.

### 4.7 The result card

Shown fields (all from the profile, month and year only for the past file):
- Header: "Past Case File TB-xxx" (the Case File id) and the owner department only if decided in question 1; then the label chip "Past Case File, not advice for this patient" (muted chip, `StatusChip kind="muted"`, same position as in `PublishedPanel.tsx:19`).
- Cancer (`protocol_label` or `cancer_name`), setting, age band and sex ("50 to 59, F"), stage with its kind chip ("as recorded", "Vaidyx draft stage, confirmed by Dr X", "as agreed by the board"), "cannot stage" if none.
- Key values: histology, grade, and the biomarkers that exist in both files first (each with its quote and where it sits, as in the Paper quote list).
- Board decisions: per record, "Board, Mar 2025: decision recorded, intent curative" then the decision text as recorded (whole, in a quote style, with "as written by the board" caption). No rewording, no highlighting of a "recommended" option.
- Outcome so far: the Case summary's follow-up sentences as stored (with their cites) and the code line about later records; or "No records after the board decision yet".
- Why similar (up to 3 lines) and How it differs (up to 2 lines), 4.6.
- A button "Open Past Case File" (4.10). No score, no percent, no stars, no bar, no "best match" word; the order is the only rank, and the help line says "Listed in order of likeness as judged by Gemma-4-31B (non-medical). The order is not a score."
What is never on the card: name, UHID/MRN, IP/OP, ABHA, accession numbers, free-text patient identity, the file names of documents, exact dates of the past file, the creating or owning doctor's name, a count of how many files resembled (a number would read as a score).
Wording rules: no "do the same", "consider", "suggest", "recommended", "treat as", "follow"; no generated treatment text anywhere in the card (the only treatment words on it are the past board's own recorded decision text and the patient's own recorded treatments, quoted). A test scans every card text for these words (6, tests).

### 4.8 What Vaidyx never does with a past decision

No model call is given a past decision as a premise for this patient. In the reasoner calls of 4.5 and 4.6 the candidate text has the "Board decisions" sentence removed (it orders by likeness of the patient, not by what was done) and the line lists exclude decision fields for "similar" pairs. The agents (Phase 6) and the Case summary builder (3.4) do not read the similar files index; a module-level guard in `similar.py` keeps its functions out of `agents`, `summary` and `postboard` imports (a test greps for imports). The card never feeds the decision form; nothing is copied into this file's record, except that the doctor may add a past file as a published-case-like note only if a later item says so (not in scope).

### 4.9 Doctor's extra words (summary of effects)

They are an extra BM25 query and an extra sentence in the dense query (4.4), appear in the reasoner prompt as "the doctor asked to look for" (4.5), are screened for identifiers and limited to 200 characters, and are written to the audit only if the owner agrees (question 4). They can promote candidates inside the cancer group; they cannot reach other cancer groups (unless the doctor ticks "search other cancers too", not proposed for v1).

### 4.10 Opening a result (D10, D8, item 1.9)

- On "Open Past Case File": the server computes `access.scope(r, past_case)`. If it is FULL or REQUESTS (a file the person may already open), the browser goes to the normal Case File page with a banner "You opened this from Similar Case Files" (audited as `similar_opened`, `via: normal_access`). Note that REQUESTS gives only a limited view; the page may offer break-glass for the rest.
- Otherwise (scope None): the break-glass dialog of item 1.9 appears with the reason pre-filled "similar to TB-xxx" (xxx = this file's id; editable, but never empty), text: "You do not have access to this Case File. You may read it, read only, for 24 hours. The reason is recorded and the owner's consultants are told." Confirm calls the doctor break-glass route (item 1.9): window of 24 h (`BREAK_GLASS_MAX_HOURS`), read-only (a doctor's own action set, for example `DOCTOR_BREAK_GLASS_ACTIONS = {view, read_case, read_audit}`: no `ask`, unlike the Vaidyx admin's `BREAK_GLASS_ACTIONS` in `access.py:41`; the agent chat and Ask are not available on a break-glass view), audited in the past file (`store.audit(..., kind="break_glass", by, reason, until, via="similar:TB-xxx")`) and in `org_audit.jsonl`, and a notification to `team.owner_consultants(past_case)` that carries no patient details and names the doctor and the reason ("Dr X opened TB-yyy read only, reason: similar to TB-xxx"; the notification text must not name the patient and must not contain this file's patient either, since "TB-xxx" is a Case File id and not a person; as the id could itself carry a name (TB-2026-bharath is such an id), the reason pre-fill uses an opaque short code when the id contains letters beyond the standard pattern; open question 5).
- A Vaidyx admin cannot use this path: `similar` routes refuse `vaidyx_admin` roles, who have a separate window; a Vaidyx admin never gets a clinical search (rule: admins read only inside their own break-glass).
- The opened page shows the read-only banner "Past Case File, not advice for this patient" and the break-glass expiry, with every write control disabled by the existing `may()` rule. The Case summary is the first thing shown. The past file's page is not editable and the Send to board and Ask about this patient actions are hidden; the agent chat and Ask are not available on a break-glass view (the route refuses `ask` too, not only the button).
- Break-glass for a protected file is impossible here: the real case is never in a result, and `access.scope` already refuses anyone but its creator.
- A resident or an outside expert may search only if they hold scope on the current file (the route requires `read_case` on this file); they cannot break the glass (a break-glass is for doctors, D8). Residents proposing a break-glass is out of scope.

### 4.11 Audit of the search itself

On every search, in this file's `audit.jsonl` (`store.audit`, `kind: "similar_search"`): `by`, `ts`, `n_candidates`, `n_results`, `cancer_group`, `model_order` (true or false), `fallback` (reason when RRF order), `words_hash` (a short hash of the doctor's words, or the words themselves under question 4), `prompt_version`. In `org_audit.jsonl` (`org.audit("similar_search", by, case_id=..., results=n, shown=[ids])`) the same minimum plus the shown ids (below). Each opened result: `similar_opened` in both files (this file: which past id; past file: who, via, reason), and the break-glass events as in 4.10. The search event in this file's `audit.jsonl` keeps only a hash of the shown id list, so the log of this file does not become a list of other patients. The list of shown past-file ids itself is written to the restricted audit: `org.audit("similar_search", by, case_id=..., results=n, shown=[ids])` in `org_audit.jsonl`, as for break-glass. Each shown past file also gets a `similar_shown` line in its own `audit.jsonl` (`by`, `ts`, `from`: the id of the file the search was made from), so its owners can see who saw it listed; never names or quotes, and never the doctor's extra words (those stay in the search event of the searching file only, under question 4). The open events record the ones that were actually opened. A failed or empty search is audited too. Nothing in the log contains patient names or identifiers.

### 4.12 Protection of the real case at every step

1. `eligible()` (4.1) refuses `config.PROTECTED_CASES` and `access.is_protected`; it is the only entry to profile building.
2. The directory scan for the index uses `store.list_cases()` and filters through `eligible()`; it never opens `data/tumour_board/cases/TB-2026-bharath` files. (The scan reads `case.json` of every directory to apply the filter, which is already what `list_cases` does; the code must check the id before reading any `docs/`, `state.json` or `audit.jsonl` of a protected directory.)
3. `refresh(case_id)` returns at once for a protected id; the startup scan, the debounce worker and the event hooks all call it.
4. At query time the candidate list is filtered through `eligible()` on live data; at card time again.
5. A searching user on the protected file itself gets "Similar Case Files is not available for this Case File" (no query leaves it either).
6. Tests (section 6): a profile for the protected id raises, the index never contains it even if a hand-made directory of that id exists in a temp data root, a result never contains it, the audit never mentions it. The e2e run on a temp `TB_DATA_ROOT` holds a copy named by the env var `TB_PROTECTED_CASES`, never the real folder.

## 5. Timing and cost

Estimates from today's code, not measured (no run was made for this note):
- Index size: a profile is about 1,500 characters of text (one or two chunks). 300 files give about 400 chunks, about 600 KB of text and about 1.2 MB of vectors (768 floats times 4 bytes each, 400 chunks is about 1.2 MB). Even 5,000 files stay near 20 MB; a plain matrix product is fine, no vector database needed.
- Build: embedding 400 short chunks on CPU with 16 threads is of the order of 1 to 2 minutes once; later only changed profiles are embedded (cache by content, `search.py:139`). Profile building is file reads and no model call: milliseconds per file for the structured part.
- Search: one query embedding (a fraction of a second, but it waits on `_emb_lock` if a rebuild batch is running; batches of 128 keep this short), BM25 over a few hundred chunks (milliseconds; `CaseIndex.bm25` is a Python loop, fine at this size, would need a sparse matrix above about 20,000 chunks), 3 listwise reranker calls of about 6,000 tokens in and about 100 out, then up to 5 pointwise line-picking calls of about 2,500 tokens in. On a single H200 shared with the reader model, the reasoner calls are the cost: a few seconds each; run the 3 permutations and the 5 line calls concurrently, so a search is about 10 to 20 seconds. Show results of stage 1 (RRF order) at once with "ordering by the model" in progress, then update.
- Cache: the final order and lines are cached per key (4.5), so repeating a search is instant.
- Background: one worker thread for profile refresh (debounced 30 s), one startup scan, nothing on a timer otherwise. Single H200 rule (`CLAUDE.md`): searches queue behind the board agents' reasoner calls; no separate service.
- Gate: the real-model run in section 6 is one GPU job at a time.

## 6. Tests

Unit (no model, temp `TB_DATA_ROOT`, synthetic cases only):
- Profile fields: from a synthetic file with known facts, board records and a summary, each structured key (4.2) is filled with the right value, source and month-year date; an unverified fact (`verification` not in `rules.VERIFIED`) is absent; a file with no stage gives `stage == []`; an agesex "54 F" gives band "50 to 59" and "F"; an unparseable agesex gives empty; no profile field or embedded text contains the patient's name, UHID, IP/OP number or file names (property test with a name seeded into every field).
- Exclusions: protected id (set via `TB_PROTECTED_CASES`) never gets a profile, never in `index.json` or `index.npz` keys, never in a result; archived files excluded and removed at once on archive; undecided files excluded (or included, per question 2); a deleted file removed; the own file excluded; an archived-after-build file is filtered at query time.
- Index store: atomic write (kill between tmp and replace leaves the old file); a changed fact changes the signature and re-embeds only that profile; unchanged profiles reuse cached vectors (count `_embed` calls with a stub).
- Candidates: the cancer-group filter keeps only the group; a generic cancer matches only the same normalised name; the doctor's words "HER2 positive with brain metastases" raise a profile quoting them over one that does not; words with a name or a UHID are refused; words longer than 200 characters are cut.
- Order: with a stub reasoner, the merge by median rank is deterministic for fixed inputs; three permutations are reproducible from the same seed; a different seed order gives the same merge when the stub is consistent; an invalid id list falls back to RRF order and sets `fallback`; the cache key changes when a profile signature changes and not otherwise; the response and the stored result contain no score or number field (schema test).
- Quote check: a pair is kept only when both quotes are found word for word (`grounding.norm`); a fuzzy match (below 100) is dropped; a quote with the past patient's name, an id, or a day-level date is dropped; an unrelated pair (different fact ids) is dropped; fewer than two lines gives the "Matched by recorded fields" line and the chip, and no unquoted text; zero lines moves the result to "Weaker matches".
- Wording: every card text of a golden set contains none of "do the same", "consider", "suggest", "recommend", "treat", "follow"; the label "Past Case File, not advice for this patient" is on every card in the JSON and in the UI test; no card has name, UHID, owner doctor or day-level dates of the past file.
- Permissions and audit: a doctor with FULL opens normally and no break-glass is written; a doctor with None gets the dialog data with reason "similar to TB-xxx" and, on confirm, a window of 24 h, read only (writes return 403), both files audited, the owner's consultants notified, the Vaidyx admin refused; a protected result is impossible; every search writes `similar_search` with counts and no names, the shown ids in `org_audit.jsonl` only, and a `similar_shown` line (who, when, from which file; no names, quotes or the doctor's words) in each shown past file; `ask` on a doctor break-glass view returns 403; the hash chain (`store.verify_audit`) stays valid.
- Import guard: `similar.py` is not imported by `agents`, `summary`, `postboard`.

Real-model check (item 7.4, one GPU job): load the ten or more decided synthetic files below, build the index, search from Kavitha's file with and without the words "HER2 positive with brain metastases" if she is a breast file (replace the words by her real markers on the day). Expected:
- the list shows files of her cancer group only, in an order the owner finds sensible;
- the pairwise check by a clinician or the owner: for 10 random pairs of results, "which would you rather see first for this patient?" compared with the model order (agreement is reported as counts, not as an accuracy claim);
- every "why similar" line is verified by the quote checker; a count of dropped lines is logged;
- three repeats give the same order (cache bypassed once to check the determinism of the model call);
- no card carries a name or UHID (scan);
- opening a result from another department triggers the dialog and writes both audits.
Evaluation has no ground truth (section 3); the owner's "would you want to see this file" judgement is the test, with the model's order hidden when shown to the judge.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. **Department on the card.** Should the card show the past file's owner department (for example "Surgical Oncology") before it is opened? It helps a doctor know whom to call but narrows who the patient might be. Proposed default: id only, department shown after opening.
2. **Which files count as "past".** Only files with a recorded board decision (proposed), or any listed, non-archived file including undecided ones? And how far back: all time (proposed, with the month and year on the card) or the last N years?
3. **Same department first?** Should results from the doctor's own department rank first, or are all ranked together on likeness only (proposed, so the department cannot distort the order)?
4. **The doctor's words in the audit.** Keep the words in the audit (clear for review, but they can hold clinical detail such as "brain metastases") or only a hash (proposed: words kept because the audit is already clinical and restricted; the owner decides)?
5. **The Case File id in the reason.** The reason "similar to TB-xxx" and the owner notice carry a Case File id. Ids like TB-2026-bharath contain a name, so should the system use an opaque short code in notices when an id is not of the standard pattern?
6. **Before opening, how much of the other department's file may a card show?** The card shows decisions and quoted report lines of a patient the doctor has no care relationship with, de-identified only as far as the rules in 4.7. Is that acceptable under D4 and D10 with the label, or should the card show only structured fields until the break-glass is taken (then "why similar" lines and decision text appear only after opening)? I recommend the owner and the hospital's data protection lead confirm this, since the DPDP Act 2023 does not clearly cover secondary use inside the same hospital.
7. **Linked cases and the same patient.** Should files linked to this one (same patient, another cancer or an earlier episode) be hidden from the list, or shown with the chip "linked to this file"? Also: a limit on how often one doctor may search or open (for example a daily count flagged to the owner), and who reviews the break-glass log and how often (hospital practice is usually sampled review).
